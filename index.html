About this tool

What is the Social Progress Index?

Over the last decade, the Social Progress Index (SPI) has established itself as a premier tool for global leaders to gain insights into the true state of a society. Its distinctive design focuses on the issues that matter most to people such as improved health, safety, education, rights, opportunity and more. The SPI is used by governments, businesses, researchers, and organizations to better allocate resources, identify risks, pinpoint opportunities and vulnerabilities among the community, and build smarter policies and business strategies.
Developed by the nonprofit Social Progress Imperative from a framework created by Michael E. Porter (Harvard Business School) and Scott Stern (MIT), the index relies exclusively on social and environmental outcome indicators: it captures the results people actually experience, not the money spent or the policies passed. The Social Progress Index has been applied to countries, states, cities, and communities around the world, demonstrating its universality and actionability.

This edition

This edition, calculated by ICIC (Initiative for a Competitive Inner City), applies the framework to 384 U.S. metropolitan statistical areas in two snapshots, 2018 and 2023, aggregating 45 indicators into 12 components and 3 dimensions. Every value is scaled from 0 to 100, where higher is always better. Download the dataset in your account under "Resources".

How to use this tool

Use the control at the top of the page to switch between the 2018 edition, the 2023 edition, and the percent change from 2018 to 2023. Metros are grouped into three size categories based on 2018 Census Bureau population estimates, and national and size-category figures are unweighted averages across metro areas. Positive changes appear in green and negative changes in red throughout the tool.

Finding how your metro changed since 2018

Every dimension, component and indicator change for a metro is on one page:
  1. Open the Metros tab and pick your metro from the alphabetical list (type its first letters to jump to it).
  2. In the Show control at the top of the page, choose Percent change.
  3. The scorecard now shows the percent change of the SPI, its 3 dimensions and 12 components; the chart below it shows the change in each of the 45 indicators. All ranks on the page become ranks by percent change.
  4. To put several metros side by side, including the change in score points, use the Compare tab.

Methodology & metadata

Framework, definitions, sources and data vintages
This tool presents the Social Progress Index (SPI) calculated by ICIC for United States metropolitan statistical areas in two editions, 2018 and 2023. The index aggregates 45 indicators into 12 components and 3 dimensions — Basic Human Needs, Foundations of Wellbeing and Opportunity — on a 0–100 scale where higher is always better; indicators measuring negative outcomes are inverted during scaling. The tool covers the 384 metropolitan areas of the 50 states and the District of Columbia. Metros are grouped into three size categories — small (fewer than 500,000 residents), medium (500,000 to 999,999) and large (1,000,000 or more) — based on 2018 Census Bureau population estimates; size-category and national figures are unweighted averages across metro areas. Metro coordinates for the map come from the Census Bureau gazetteer. Each edition draws on the most recent data available at the time, so the "data vintage" columns below show the actual reference year of every indicator in the 2018 and 2023 calculations. Percent changes compare the 2023 edition against the 2018 edition for each measure.
